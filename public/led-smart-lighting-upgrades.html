LED & Smart Lighting Upgrades

The right lighting transforms how a home looks and feels — while cutting your energy bill. Here's how modern LED and smart lighting can upgrade your space.

Lighting is one of the most overlooked upgrades in any home — and one of the most powerful. The right lighting can make a dated room feel fresh and modern, a small space feel larger, and a long Northern Michigan winter evening feel warm and inviting. Best of all, today's LED and smart lighting does it while using a fraction of the energy of the old bulbs it replaces.

Whether you're remodeling a kitchen, finishing a basement, or simply tired of dim, uneven lighting, a thoughtfully designed lighting upgrade delivers a huge return in both comfort and style. In this guide, Matt Grace and the team at Grace Electric walk through the most popular lighting upgrades and how they can transform your Traverse City home.

Why Upgrade to LED?

If your home still runs on incandescent or older fluorescent bulbs, switching to LED is one of the easiest wins in home improvement. Modern LEDs offer real advantages:

  • Dramatic energy savings. LEDs use up to 80% less electricity than incandescent bulbs, which adds up fast across a whole home.
  • Long lifespan. Quality LEDs can last 15–25 years, meaning you rarely change a bulb again.
  • Better light quality. Choose the exact color temperature you want — warm and cozy or bright and crisp.
  • Less heat. LEDs run cool, which is safer and eases the load on your A/C in summer.
  • Instant, flicker-free light even in the cold — a real plus for garages and porches up north.

Popular Lighting Upgrades That Make a Big Difference

Beyond simply swapping bulbs, these upgrades can genuinely change how your home looks and functions:

  • Recessed LED lighting. Clean, modern "can" or wafer lights that wash a room in even, glare-free light — perfect for kitchens, living rooms, and finished basements.
  • Dimmer switches. Set the perfect mood in any room and save even more energy.
  • Under-cabinet lighting. A kitchen favorite that adds both function and a high-end look.
  • Pendant lights and chandeliers. Statement fixtures over islands, dining tables, and entryways.
  • Outdoor and landscape lighting. Boost curb appeal, safety, and security with pathway, accent, and motion-sensor lighting.
  • Closet, pantry, and task lighting. Small additions that make daily life noticeably easier.

Going Smart: Lighting You Can Control From Anywhere

Smart lighting takes convenience and efficiency a step further. With smart switches, dimmers, and fixtures, you gain control that traditional lighting simply can't match:

  • Control from your phone or voice — turn lights on or off from bed, the couch, or across the country.
  • Schedules and automation — have lights come on at sunset or wake you gently in the morning.
  • Away-mode security — make your home look lived-in while you're traveling or up at the cottage.
  • Scenes and zones — one tap sets the perfect lighting for dinner, movie night, or entertaining.
  • Energy insights — many systems help you track and reduce usage.

Remodeling a room? Lighting is far easier and cheaper to upgrade while walls or ceilings are already open. Planning your lighting early is one of the smartest moves in any renovation.

Lighting Room by Room

Great lighting isn't one-size-fits-all — each room has its own job to do. Here's how the right approach changes from space to space:

  • Kitchen. Combine recessed lighting for overall brightness, under-cabinet lighting for counters, and pendants over the island for style.
  • Living room. Layer recessed lighting with dimmers and accent fixtures to shift from bright and functional to warm and relaxing.
  • Bedrooms. Soft, dimmable lighting plus good bedside task lighting makes the room comfortable and practical.
  • Bathrooms. Even, shadow-free lighting around the vanity, with proper moisture-rated fixtures.
  • Basements and garages. Bright, efficient LED fixtures that turn dim, underused spaces into ones you actually enjoy.
  • Exterior. Pathway, accent, and security lighting for safety and curb appeal through long northern nights.

Choosing the Right Color Temperature

One of the biggest reasons a lighting upgrade succeeds or disappoints is color temperature, measured in Kelvin (K). Picking the right tone for each space makes all the difference:

  • Warm white (2700K–3000K). Cozy and inviting — ideal for living rooms, bedrooms, and dining areas.
  • Neutral white (3500K–4000K). Clean and balanced — great for kitchens, bathrooms, and workspaces.
  • Cool white (5000K+). Bright and crisp — best for garages, workshops, and task-heavy areas.

We'll help you choose the right temperature for each room, and even tunable options that let you adjust the tone whenever you like.

Why Professional Installation Is Worth It

While swapping a bulb is a DIY job, adding recessed lighting, new fixtures, dimmers, or smart switches involves wiring, ceilings, and sometimes new circuits. A licensed electrician makes sure it's done right:

  • Fixtures and cans are placed for even, balanced light — no dark corners or hot spots
  • Wiring and dimmers are matched correctly to avoid flicker and buzz
  • New circuits are added safely where needed
  • Everything is installed to code and built to last

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you add recessed lighting to a finished ceiling?

Yes. Adding recessed lights to an existing finished ceiling is one of our most common jobs. We work carefully to fish wiring and cut clean openings, keeping patching and mess to a minimum.

Will new LED lighting really lower my energy bill?

It can make a noticeable difference, especially in homes with a lot of older bulbs. Because LEDs use up to 80% less energy and last for years, the savings add up over time — on both your power bill and replacement bulbs.

Do smart lights require special wiring?

Most smart switches and fixtures work with standard wiring, though some benefit from a neutral wire at the switch. We'll check what your home has and recommend the best option so everything works reliably.

How many recessed lights does a room need?

It depends on the room's size, ceiling height, and how you use the space. Too few leaves dark spots; too many can feel harsh. Getting the spacing and placement right is exactly where a professional lighting plan pays off — and it's something we handle for every job.
